A simple Japanese language drilling app

Andrew Brittle, 2026

I started putting this together as a simple tool to help me drill Japanese — I have a trip to Japan coming up, and I've tried to learn hiragana and katakana a few times and failed miserably. This time I did it as part of Japanese language lessons. As the lessons went on I expanded it to cover the vocabulary we were covering in class, and started building some extra bits for myself that were outside the scope of the lessons.

What you see here is that development to date. It's changing all the time and new bits are being added, but in theory you can turn off all of the "Andrew mode" stuff and focus just on what you need to.

Settings

Light theme
Switches from a dark to light theme.
Show kanji content
At the beginning of this project I collected together a small set of survival kanji, as things I might encounter on my trip. The list grew and eventually there was significant overlap with N5 kanji. The collection here is aligned with N5 but doesn't include "white" — there was nowhere to put it at the time.
Show Extra content
Shows the extra categories I've added for myself. Initially colours, "useful words for the trip", and more recently "UK Places". UK Places is really just to supplement the selection of places we've come across in the lessons.
Show "Themes"
This might be an interesting one as it develops. The normal reading challenges work on individual categories in one level of the course. Themes can go across levels and can also include words from the Extra categories — things like "Travel and UK places", "Around the world" (countries and cities), "Days and times" (vocab associated with both groups), and so on. If you have Extra turned off, the Extra words won't be included in the drilling.
Half credit for hinted answers
All drilling offers an audio clip to help you answer, but some people might think that's cheating. If you agree, you can penalise yourself by only getting half marks if you listen to a hint before answering. You can still listen as many times as you like afterwards.
Work offline
Caches the application so it doesn't need to be online to work. It's a very small app so this doesn't really get in the way. It's a Progressive Web App, which means it's just a webpage that tries to pretend it's not in a browser.
Activate Andrew mode
Whatever mad scheme I'm working on will be visible by turning this on. I'm unlikely to be taking anything away from the app, so it's probably new stuff that gets enabled by this.

Keyboard

Not a setting, but if you're using the app on something with a physical keyboard: you can use 1–4 to select the cards, and space to move to the next one.

Scoring

In the background everything is scored 0.5 for a correct answer and -0.75 for an incorrect one. The lower limit for scoring is -2 and the upper limit is +2. The score will show as a shade of red or green (or blue if it's back to zero again).

Hiragana drilling

Select whether you want the base set of characters, dakuten/handakuten, or contracted.

There's a handy reference card to show the kana and corresponding dakuten kana.

You can either select the whole set for drilling, or individual rows.

There's a toggle before you start to drill that lets you decide whether to have the kana character as the big one on screen and choose from 4 romaji options, or romaji as the big character and choose from 4 hiragana.

You can click to hear an audio clip.

You'll get your score at the end of the session and an option to either drill the same overall set of characters again, or just the ones you got wrong.

Katakana drilling

Select whether you want the base set of characters, dakuten/handakuten, or contracted.

There's a handy reference card to show the kana and corresponding dakuten kana.

You can either select the whole set for drilling, or individual rows.

There's a toggle before you start to drill that lets you decide whether to have the kana character as the big one on screen and choose from 4 romaji options, or romaji as the big character and choose from 4 katakana.

There's another toggle that will replace the romaji completely and only show a comparison between katakana and hiragana. It felt like it might be a nice challenge to remove the romaji from this, if you feel brave.

You can click to hear an audio clip.

Kanji drilling

This will only appear if you have kanji switched on.

Select which set you want — numbers, nature, travel, food, places, people, general, verbs.

You can either select individual kanji or the whole set for drilling. Yes, you can game the system by selecting one and continuously drilling it, because there's only one right answer. But that's just letting yourself down.

There's another toggle that will replace the romaji with English and only show a large kanji, with the options shown in English and hiragana — so there's no romaji to immediately match with the audio clip.

You can click to hear an audio clip.

You'll get your score at the end of the session and an option to either drill the same overall set of characters again, or just the ones you got wrong.

Vocab

This has a few options.

Read

Reading numbers — presents 10 random numbers. These are mostly 1–9,999 but there's a 1 in 20 chance of a bigger number up to 50,000, to practice man. Correct answers score 0.1, incorrect answers score -0.1.

Reading times — presents 10 times with from/until/at and around qualifiers, and am/pm indicators. Correct answers score 0.1, incorrect answers score -0.1.

Specific time — instead of a random time, you can put an actual time in here together with the qualifiers, and it will tell you how that time would be read. It starts with the current time and has a refresh button.

Reading words — everything — picks 20 random words from across all categories. Works the same way as everything else.

Reading kanji — everything — picks 20 random kanji from across all categories. Works the same way as everything else.

Highlight the meaning instead of sound — sounds complicated but it just changes the emphasis on the four answer cards. By default you could look at the kana and think "that starts with 'mi'" and guess the word from there by looking at the four romaji alternatives and choosing the one where the it starts with 'mi'. Highlighting the meaning, in English, means you can read and recognise the word but to get it right you need to know the meaning as well – it starts with "mi", but what does it mean? The romaji is still there down below and slightly less visible so it doesn't actually stop you doing it the easy way, but it's a mild deterrent.

Reverse the drill direction — shows the English word as the big word and four kana words as the options.
