A simple Japanese language drilling app

Andrew Brittle, 2026

I started putting this together as a simple tool to help me drill Japanese — I have a trip to Japan coming up, and I've tried to learn hiragana and katakana a few times and failed miserably. This time I did it as part of Japanese language lessons. As the lessons went on I expanded it to cover the vocabulary we were covering in class, and started building some extra bits for myself that were outside the scope of the lessons.

What you see here is that development to date. It's changing all the time and new bits are being added, but in theory you can turn off all of the "Andrew mode" stuff and focus just on what you need to.

Settings

Light theme
Switches from a dark to light theme.
Show kanji content
At the beginning of this project I collected together a small set of survival kanji, as things I might encounter on my trip. The list grew and eventually there was significant overlap with N5 kanji. The collection here is aligned with N5 but doesn't include "white" — there was nowhere to put it at the time. You can turn this off if you don't want to see it.
Half credit for hinted answers
All drilling offers an audio clip to help you answer, but some people might think that's cheating. If you agree, you can penalise yourself by only getting half marks if you listen to a hint before answering. You can still listen as many times as you like afterwards.
Work offline
Caches the application so it doesn't need to be online to work. It's a very small app so this doesn't really get in the way. It's a Progressive Web App, which means it's just a webpage that tries to pretend it's not in a browser.
Trip vocabulary
This has been added as various piles of words that might be useful for me to know for my trip. This entire section will only appear if you turn it on — otherwise the whole app will act as if none of this exists.
Activate Andrew mode
Whatever mad scheme I'm working on will be visible by turning this on. Currently holds "Materials" — vocab pulled from photos I've actually taken on the trip (signs, menus, receipts, and so on), plus a couple of linguistic notes pages. More will turn up here as the trip goes on. It's turned off by default.

Keyboard

Not a setting, but if you're using the app on something with a physical keyboard: you can use 1–4 to select the cards, and space to move to the next one.

Scoring

In the background everything is scored 0.5 for a correct answer and -0.75 for an incorrect one. The lower limit for scoring is -2 and the upper limit is +2. The score will show as a shade of red or green (or blue if it's back to zero again).

Hiragana drilling

Select whether you want the base set of characters, dakuten/handakuten, or contracted.

There's a handy reference card to show the kana and corresponding dakuten kana.

You can either select the whole set for drilling, or individual rows.

There's a toggle before you start to drill that lets you decide whether to have the kana character as the big one on screen and choose from 4 romaji options, or romaji as the big character and choose from 4 hiragana.

You can click to hear an audio clip.

You'll get your score at the end of the session and an option to either drill the same overall set of characters again, or just the ones you got wrong.

Kanji drilling

This will only appear if you have kanji switched on.

Select which set you want — numbers, nature, travel, food, places, people, general, verbs.

You can either select individual kanji or the whole set for drilling. Yes, you can game the system by selecting one and continuously drilling it, because there's only one right answer. But that's just letting yourself down.

There's another toggle that will replace the romaji with English and only show a large kanji, with the options shown in English and hiragana — so there's no romaji to immediately match with the audio clip.

Numbers

This brings together some functions that deal with reading numbers, times, and counters.

Reading numbers

Presents 10 random numbers. These are mostly 1–9,999 but there's a 1 in 20 chance of a bigger number up to 50,000, to practice man. Correct answers score 0.1, incorrect answers score -0.1.

Specific number

Instead of a random number, you can put an actual number in here and it will tell you how that number would be read.

Reading times

Presents 10 times with from/until/at and around qualifiers, and am/pm indicators. Correct answers score 0.1, incorrect answers score -0.1.

Specific time

Instead of a random time, you can put an actual time in here together with the qualifiers, and it will tell you how that time would be read. It starts with the current time and has a refresh button.

Counters

Random counter challenge — pick exactly four counter types, then work through 10 rounds reading a romaji phrase like "six eggs" and matching it to the right counter. Correct answers score 0.1, incorrect answers score -0.1, the same as Reading numbers and Reading times.

There's also a couple of reference pages for different types of counters — one for people, objects, and animals, and one for minutes/hours/days/weeks/months/years. I know we haven't covered these in the lessons but they're easily ignored and don't feed into anything else — I was just curious.

Specific counter — choose a counter type and enter a number (1–50, or 1–10 for つ, since native Japanese counting stops at とお) and it'll show you how it's written.

You don't have to turn this off if you don't want it — just ignore it.

Money

An editable GBP↔JPY exchange rate — starts off around the going rate, but you can update it any time — plus a two-way currency converter: put in a value in either currency and see the other straight away.

Alongside the conversion there's also a tax-free price, working out what you'd pay with Japan's 10% consumption tax taken off (¥ ÷ 1.1) — handy for checking whether a price you're looking at already has tax included.

Vocab

This has a few options.

Search

There's a search pill next to the level tabs (it takes the full width unless Andrew mode is switched on, in which case the Andrew pill moves to its right). Type at least two characters and press enter — it only searches when you submit, not as you type. It matches the start of any word in the English meaning or the romaji, so partial words still work ("cred" finds "credit card") but coincidental letter runs in the middle of a word don't ("up" won't match inside "supermarket" just because the letters happen to sit next to each other). It only searches real taught or added vocabulary, never the extra background sentences shown as context on some Materials cards. Results respect whatever you've currently got switched on — Trip and Andrew words only turn up if those are on — and take you to the normal selection screen to drill whichever ones you pick, colour-coded by which level they're from.

Collections

The normal reading challenges work on individual categories in one level of the course. Collections can go across levels and can also include words from any additional categories that you've got turned on. This pulls together combinations like "All vocabulary" and "All kanji" (if you have it turned on) as well as more specific collections of categories like "Transport and UK Places", "Around the world" (countries and cities), "Days and times" (vocab associated with both groups), and so on.

Note that the size of the collection will depend on whether you're just using vocab from the lesson or including other categories too.

All vocabulary — picks 20 random words from across all categories that are visible to you. Works the same way as everything else.

All kanji — picks 20 random words from across all kanji categories, if you have kanji turned on. Works the same way as everything else.

Other collections include:

  • Transport and UK Places
  • Around the world
  • Calendar & time — Days of the week, Months, Days of the month, and Time vocabulary, kept in their taught order rather than alphabetized (they're ordinal sequences, so sorting them would just scramble them)
  • Everyday items, clothing & general vocab
  • Core grammar words — Verbs, Adjectives, and Question & location words: the "functional" categories that build a sentence, rather than themed nouns
  • Question words — every word tagged as a question word, wherever it actually lives, rather than a fixed list of categories. Right now that's a handful already taught in Question & location words plus a set pulled from an Instagram reference graphic (only in Andrew mode). Nothing's duplicated — a word that's taught in the lessons and also tagged here is still just one word with one score, it just shows up in both places. If Andrew mode is off, this collection quietly shrinks to only the taught ones rather than disappearing outright.

And if you have Trip vocabulary turned on too you'll also see "Getting around" and "Survival vocab".

Highlight the meaning instead of sound — sounds complicated but it just changes the emphasis on the four answer cards. By default you could look at the kana and think "that starts with 'mi'" and guess the word from there by looking at the four romaji alternatives and choosing the one where the it starts with 'mi'. Highlighting the meaning, in English, means you can read and recognise the word but to get it right you need to know the meaning as well – it starts with "mi", but what does it mean? The romaji is still there down below and slightly less visible so it doesn't actually stop you doing it the easy way, but it's a mild deterrent.

Reverse the drill direction — shows the English word as the big word and four kana words as the options.

Grammar notes

A "Grammar notes" card sits above the Level 2 category list, gathering every sentence/particle reference in one place rather than leaving them scattered near whichever category happened to be nearby when each one was added:

  • を — the object particle, with the one exception in this batch called out (あいます, "to meet", takes に instead)
  • で — its two separate jobs: the method or tool used to get somewhere (でんしゃで, "by train"), and where an action takes place — easily confused with に, which marks where something exists or a destination, never where an action happens
  • だれも/どこも/なにも + negative — "nobody" / "nowhere" / "nothing", flags いつ as the one exception (いつも is a fixed idiom meaning "always", not part of this pattern)
  • Sentence order (motion sentences) — breaks down the standard sentence order for simple motion sentences (topic/time/companion/means/destination/verb), including a shuffle button that builds random example sentences from the app's own vocabulary
  • Sentence order (を + object) — the same tap-to-cycle, shuffle-a-new-example format, but for the genuinely different pattern that を introduces: topic/time/location-of-action/object/verb, with each object-taking verb paired only with objects that actually make sense with it (no "drink a photograph")
  • Verb tense & affirmative/negative — two cards, one for the original nine verbs and one for the newer object-taking set, each switchable with pills at the top (sorted alphabetically by meaning) and each verb showing its たい ("want to") form alongside the plain ます-form, since たい conjugates differently (like an adjective, not a verb)

Andrew mode

Only appears if you've got it switched on. Whatever bits and pieces I'm testing at the moment. Most things here will eventually move to a proper home in the app, but can still be turned off if you want just the lessons.

  • Materials — Appliances reference — a lookup-only page (not drillable on its own) grouping words from the Appliances & machines category below by which device they were actually seen on: air conditioning, TV remote, laundry (the washing machine's own panel plus things like detergent bottle labels), microwave, door lock/key card. The idea is "my remote has a button that looks like this" recognition rather than a fresh set of things to test on — the words themselves stay in Appliances & machines, this is just a filtered, grouped view over that same list, so nothing's duplicated or scored separately. Words that aren't literally on a panel/button/dispenser (payment logistics, warning text, and so on) aren't tagged, so they don't show up here.
  • Materials — Stations & trains

  • Materials — Restaurants: Ordering & policy
  • Materials — Shops & everyday purchases
  • Materials — Entertainment & sightseeing
  • Materials — Hotel: Appliances & machines
  • Materials — Hotel: Facilities & stay
  • Materials — Street & neighbourhood — signage and notices run into incidentally while wandering a town on foot (historical plaques, shopfronts, community noticeboards), as opposed to the other Materials buckets which are all places gone to on purpose

These are real words and phrases pulled from photos I've taken on the trip itself — signs, menus, receipts, announcements — rather than textbook vocabulary. Where a word was seen as part of a longer sentence or sign, that context shows on the drill card itself (and again after answering) since it's just background that might help it stick, not something to test on. Each word also notes roughly where and when it was photographed. Cards work the same way as Level 1/2/Trip: tap a category, pick which words to drill (or select them all), then go.
